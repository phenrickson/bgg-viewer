What the filter bar buys

Full width for the row, which is where designer and publisher become legible. For an unreleased game those two fields plus the categories are everything you know — 99.7% of upcoming games have a publisher, 83% a designer.

What it costs: a second structural idiom, and the facet lists collapse into dropdowns that hide their counts. Card Game 612 in Layout A's rail is itself a fact about the year.

Four numbers went away

Predicted average, predicted ratings count, predicted complexity-as-a-number, and hurdle-as-a-column are all gone from the row. They live on the game's detail page, whose prediction panel already renders all five in full — which is the right place for someone who came to ask about the model.

What survives is the rank, one word, and one meter.

Discover's meter domain — 5.5 to 8.8

Fixed on purpose, so a meter means the same thing on every page of the app.

A predictions domain — 4.5 to 7.0

Same twenty-two games, rescaled to the band they actually occupy.

The one encoding that breaks

Predicted geek rating runs 5.0 to 6.93 across all 4,842 upcoming games, median 5.46. On the 5.5–8.8 domain that GameRow and RatingBar both hardcode, over half the population renders as an empty meter and the whole set sits inside the left 43%.

It matters more now than it did when the row carried five numbers: this meter is the only visual signal left. Either the page gets its own domain — and a meter here stops meaning what a meter on /discover means — or the meter goes and expected rating is a bare number.

Open

Not settled here

Sort control — a single select rather than sortable headers, since there are no headers. Expected rating is the default; year and name are the obvious alternates.

Cover art — the initials stub is inherited from Discover. ~4,800 thumbnail URLs is a far smaller artifact than the 38k Discover was measured against, and this page would use it well.

Whether 2027/2028 share the page — 494 games between them, thin on categories and designers.

The hurdle control's label — "Likely to be rated" is a placeholder for a filter that most users should never need to touch.
