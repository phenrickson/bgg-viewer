What the rail buys

/predictions and /games read as the same kind of place: rail on the left, count and chips above the answer, list below. Muscle memory transfers, and the facet components (FacetList, EntityFilter) drop in unmodified.

What it costs: six controls in a 14rem column that Explore fills with fifteen. The rail runs out of content well before the list runs out of rows, and the list gives up ~14rem of width — which is where the metadata line under each title gets truncated.

What the filter bar buys

The list runs the full page width, so the metadata line under each title — designer, publisher, categories, the only things you actually know about an unreleased game — has room to say all three instead of ellipsing after the designer.

What it costs: a second structural idiom in the app, and the facet controls collapse from always-visible lists with counts into dropdowns that hide what's available until opened. The counts in Layout A's rail (Card Game 612) are themselves information about the year.

Explore's domain — 5.5 to 8.8

Fixed on purpose, so a mark means the same thing on every page of the app.

A predictions domain — 4.5 to 7.0

Same twenty-two games, rescaled to the band they actually occupy.

The fork

Predicted geek rating runs 5.0 to 6.93 across all 4,842 upcoming games, with a median of 5.46. On Explore's domain the whole set is pinned into the left 43% of the track — visible in the left panel, where the top of the list sits at the same place as its middle and the scale reads as saying nothing.

Giving the page its own domain makes the column legible, and breaks the rule the encoding was built on: a mark on /predictions would no longer sit where a mark on /games sits. The alternative is to keep one domain and drop the mark here, leaving predicted geek as a bare number.

What the real data argues, unprompted

Row 12, Ashes & Amber, and row 18, Ortomnia: Broken Trinity — both rank in the top twenty by predicted geek rating, and both carry a hurdle under 0.45. Ortomnia has no designer listed and a publisher of "(Web published)". They are in the list above because the default hurdle filter is off in these screens; at the shipped default of 0.25 the second one drops out.

That is the argument for hurdle being a visible column and not only a filter: it is the thing that explains why a row that looks strong is not.
